CONCLUSION: PAD depth is ~10.3 dB and CONSTANT.  The apparent decline is
not a function of gain -- it is a function of the level reaching the
preamp input.  Aligning the two runs by PAD-off level rather than by
gain shows the correspondence exactly:

   master 64, gain 20  ->  off -37.29  depth 9.71
   master  8, gain 40  ->  off -37.48  depth 9.72
   master 64, gain 30  ->  off -29.73  depth 8.81
   master  8, gain 50  ->  off -29.85  depth 8.83
   master 64, gain 40  ->  off -23.74  depth 6.99
   master  8, gain 60  ->  off -23.78  depth 7.03

Three pairs at different gain settings, matching to 0.04 dB.  Dropping
the master by 18 dB slid the whole curve two gain steps along.

The quietest points converge on 10.29 / 10.27 / 10.22 dB.  RME publish
"-11 dB up to +65 dB" as the gain range, so ~10.3 dB measured is
consistent with the PAD being the -11 dB bottom of that range.

WHAT THIS IMPLIES ABOUT THE MEASUREMENT RIG

The compression tracks the level at the PREAMP INPUT, not the preamp
output.  That reconciles three things that looked contradictory:

  1. The loop-linearity test (0.994 dB/dB) ran at master 4 with 64 dB of
     gain: high OUTPUT level but a tiny input, so no compression.
  2. The cable gain sweep's taper over its top five register steps
     appeared only in the cable rig, and the no-cable noise measurement
     -- which has no input signal at all -- shows no taper there.
  3. The unexplained master 128 -> 256 jump into a full-scale square
     wave happened with PAD OFF: the input stage running out of
     headroom and hard-clipping.  Exactly the overload the PAD exists
     to prevent.

So: the cable rig is only trustworthy while the preamp input is low.
The no-cable noise method has no input signal and is not subject to
this at all, which is why it is the one to publish.
